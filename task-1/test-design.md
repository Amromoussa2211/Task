# Task 1 — Test Design & Risk Coverage

**Feature:** Local Fund Transfer (mobile banking app)
**Requirement source:** BA requirement with six acceptance criteria (AC1–AC6)
**Suggested time:** 40 minutes | **Actual time:** 55 minutes

---

## 1.1 Questions and Assumptions

| # | Question | Why it matters | Assumption if unanswered |
|---|----------|----------------|--------------------------|
| 1 | Is the 20,000 daily limit per customer aggregated across all source accounts, or per account? | Aggregation scope determines how limit-exceeded tests are designed and which account(s) the limit service is queried against. A per-account limit would let a customer move 20,000 from each of three accounts — a very different risk profile. | Per-customer, aggregated across all source accounts. |
| 2 | Is the 10,000 per-transaction limit applied to the transfer amount alone, or to amount + fee? | If the fee is added on top and pushes the total debit above 10,000, a transfer of 9,990 with a 50 fee would breach the limit. The boundary test at 10,000 would be wrong. | Limit applies to the transfer amount before fee. |
| 3 | How is the transfer fee calculated — fixed amount, percentage of the transfer, tiered by amount band, or tiered by customer segment? | Fee logic is a high-risk area. A flat 5 SAR fee, a 1% percentage fee, and a tiered structure each need different test data and different boundary cases. The fee is also a source of rounding discrepancies. | Fee is a configurable value from the bank's fee configuration; tested as data-driven from a config table. |
| 4 | What is the OTP expiry window, and is it counted from generation or from first display to the customer? | The expiry window defines the boundary for expired-OTP tests. Counting from generation vs. display changes the effective window the customer sees. | 5 minutes from generation. |
| 5 | Is there an OTP retry limit before the transfer is locked, and if so what is it and what is the lockout duration? | Lockout behaviour is a security and usability concern. A customer locked out of transferring needs a documented recovery path. Without a known limit, the test approach is exploratory rather than targeted. | 5 attempts, 15-minute lockout. |
| 6 | Is the OTP bound to the specific transfer payload (amount, source account, beneficiary), or only to the user session? | If bound only to the session, a captured OTP could be replayed on a different transfer. Binding to the payload is the safer design and changes how we test OTP reuse. | OTP is bound to the specific transfer request. |
| 7 | How is transfer idempotency enforced — by a client-generated request ID, by deduplicating the full payload within a time window, or by locking the UI during OTP entry? | Idempotency design determines whether a double-tap on "Confirm" produces one debit or two. This is a top financial-risk area and the test approach depends entirely on the mechanism. | Idempotent by request identity within a time window; a duplicate request with the same identity returns the original result. |
| 8 | What is the atomicity guarantee if the SMS gateway fails after the debit is committed? Is there a compensation/reversal, or is the transfer left as committed with an incident logged? | This determines whether SMS failure is a rollback trigger or a non-rollback side-effect. The test for SMS failure then checks either for rollback or for the transfer being committed without the SMS. | SMS is a post-commit side-effect; failure does not roll back the transfer. |
| 9 | What happens to a transfer in progress if the beneficiary is deactivated or deleted by another channel during the OTP window? | This is a concurrency edge case. A debit with no valid destination is a financial and compliance risk. The behaviour could be a clean failure, a pending state that resolves later, or a silent success with no credit. | Beneficiary status is re-validated at confirmation time; if invalid, the transfer fails with no debit. |
| 10 | What currency does the Local Fund Transfer feature operate in, and is multi-currency supported at this stage? | Currency affects amount validation, fee calculation, and whether there are FX integration points. A single-currency feature is simpler; multi-currency introduces conversion and precision concerns. | Single native currency per account; cross-currency is out of scope for this feature. |
| 11 | What is the daily limit reset time — midnight in the customer's local timezone, midnight UTC, or a fixed hour? | Reset timing affects when a customer can transfer again and how limit tests are sequenced across midnight. A UTC reset would let a customer in a +6 timezone transfer earlier than expected. | Midnight in the customer's local timezone. |
| 12 | Are transaction reference numbers generated by the transfer service or by a shared sequencing service, and are they guaranteed globally unique? | Reference uniqueness is a compliance and reconciliation concern. A collision can cause audit failures and customer confusion. The test approach depends on whether uniqueness is within the transfer service or across the bank. | References are globally unique, date-prefixed with a sequence number. |
| 13 | What is the rounding rule for fee calculation when the result is not a whole cent (e.g., 1.5% of 1,234.56)? | Rounding differences can cause reconciliation failures between the app, the ledger, and the fee system. The test must assert against the correct rounded value, not the raw calculation. | Half-up rounding to two decimal places. |
| 14 | Is there a cut-off time after which local transfers are queued for next-day processing rather than executed immediately? | A cut-off introduces a pending state and changes the happy-path timeline. It also creates a boundary test at the cut-off hour. | No cut-off; transfers execute immediately. |
| 15 | What error messages does the customer see for each failure mode — specific (e.g., "Insufficient funds") or generic ("Transfer failed")? | Generic errors hide root causes and make customer support and bug triage harder. Specific, distinct messages are also a testable assertion: each failure mode should produce its own message. | Specific, distinct messages per failure mode. |

## 1.2 Risk Assessment

Risk ranking is by **likelihood × financial/regulatory impact**, not by ease of
testing. A low-likelihood, high-impact risk (e.g., duplicate debit) ranks above
a high-likelihood, low-impact risk (e.g., a confusing error message).

| # | Risk area | What could go wrong | Customer impact | Bank impact | Impact on delivery partner |
|---|-----------|---------------------|-----------------|-------------|----------------------------|
| 1 | **Duplicate debit / idempotency failure** | A double-tap on Confirm, a retry after a timeout, or a replayed OTP produces two debits for one transfer. | Customer loses twice the intended amount; recovery is slow and visible. | Direct financial loss; potential regulatory report if material; reputational damage. | Blamed for a visible money-loss bug; likely given to QA as a P1 post-release defect. |
| 2 | **Limit bypass via concurrency** | Two transfers initiated nearly simultaneously each see a remaining daily limit above zero and both succeed, collectively exceeding 20,000. | Customer exceeds their agreed limit; possible overdraft or policy breach. | Limit control failure; aggregated daily exposure exceeds tolerance; possible regulatory reporting threshold breach. | Seen as a controls failure; likely escalated to the bank's risk team. |
| 3 | **OTP payload binding missing / replayable OTP** | An OTP is bound only to the session, not the transfer. A user (or attacker) captures an OTP and applies it to a different transfer. | Customer's funds transferred to an unintended beneficiary. | Fraud-enabling gap; potential for customer disputes and chargeback-like claims. | Severe reputation impact; may trigger a fraud review and delay the release. |
| 4 | **Atomicity of debit + fee** | The debit succeeds but the fee deduction fails (or vice versa), leaving the account in an inconsistent state — wrong balance, missing fee, or un-reversed debit. | Customer balance does not match the expected post-transfer figure; reconciliation fails. | Ledger discrepancy; fee revenue loss or double-charging; audit trail gap. | Finance/operations team raises a reconciliation incident; QA owns the root-cause investigation. |
| 5 | **Insufficient-funds edge cases** | A transfer is attempted with a balance that is exactly equal to the amount, or with a balance that covers the amount but not the amount + fee. The transfer succeeds when it should not, or fails without a clear message. | Customer overdraws or is blocked confusingly. | Overdraft exposure; customer complaints; potential fee income loss if transfers are wrongly blocked. | Seen as a product correctness issue; affects customer trust in the app. |
| 6 | **Beneficiary validation at confirmation time** | A beneficiary is deleted, blocked, or frozen between selection and OTP confirmation. The transfer proceeds to a non-valid destination. | Funds sent to a frozen or wrong account; recovery is complex and may be irreversible. | Regulatory concern if funds leave the bank to an invalid destination; potential frozen-asset handling. | High-severity incident; likely halts the release until understood. |
| 7 | **Transaction reference generation** | Reference numbers collide, are not unique, or are predictable in a way that enables fraud. | Customer cannot reference their transfer; duplicate references cause confusion in disputes. | Audit trail weakness; compliance risk if references are not traceable. | Compliance and audit teams may require a redesign of the reference scheme. |
| 8 | **SMS dependency as a transfer trigger** | The transfer is not committed until the SMS is sent, and the SMS gateway is slow or unavailable, causing transfers to fail or hang. **OR** the transfer commits before the SMS and the SMS never arrives, leaving the customer unaware. | Customer does not receive confirmation; may attempt to re-transfer and double-debit. | Customer confusion, support load, potential duplicate transfers if the customer retries. | Support cost and customer-trust impact; not a financial loss if the transfer is committed correctly. |
| 9 | **Fee calculation error** | Fee is calculated incorrectly (wrong percentage, wrong tier, rounding error) and is either under- or over-charged. | Customer charged the wrong amount; overcharge creates a complaint, undercharge creates revenue loss. | Revenue leakage or customer redress cost; reconciliation discrepancy. | Finance raises a revenue discrepancy; QA is drawn into a root-cause review. |
| 10 | **Error taxonomy — generic errors masking root cause** | Multiple failure modes return the same generic message ("Transfer failed"). | Customer cannot tell why the transfer failed; support cannot triage without logs. | Higher support cost; slower resolution; potential for customers to retry incorrectly. | Seen as a product-quality gap; not financial but affects satisfaction and support load. |
| 11 | **Daily limit reset timing mismatch** | Reset uses UTC instead of customer local time, letting the customer transfer earlier or later than their agreed daily window. | Customer can transfer outside the intended window (or is blocked earlier than expected). | Daily limit control is applied at the wrong boundary; aggregated exposure may be miscalculated. | Risk/compliance may flag the mismatch; minor compared to a limit bypass. |
| 12 | **Amount validation gaps** | Negative amounts, zero amounts, excessively large amounts, or amounts with more than two decimal places are accepted and processed. | Erroneous transfer attempted; may result in a debit/credit anomaly. | Data integrity issue in the ledger; potential for unexpected account states. | Seen as input-validation weakness; moderate severity, easy to fix. |

### Ranking logic

The top four risks (duplicate debit, limit bypass, OTP replay, atomicity) all
involve **money moving incorrectly or twice**. They share a high likelihood of
being edge cases that are easy to miss in happy-path testing, and a high
financial or regulatory impact if they occur. They are ranked above SMS
dependency and fee calculation because those are either non-rollback side-effects
or revenue discrepancies that are easier to detect and reverse. Error taxonomy
and amount validation are real but lower-impact — they degrade the experience
and create support load rather than causing direct financial loss.

## 1.3 Test Cases

Maximum 12. Each case targets a distinct root cause.

| ID | Title | Preconditions | Steps | Expected result |
|----|-------|---------------|-------|-----------------|
| TC-01 | Happy path — full local transfer with all fields | Customer is logged in; customer has at least one source account with sufficient balance; at least one active beneficiary is registered; daily limit not exhausted; fee configuration is loaded | 1. Navigate to Local Fund Transfer. 2. Select a source account. 3. Select a registered beneficiary. 4. Enter an amount of 1,000 (below both limits). 5. Enter a purpose note. 6. Tap Continue. 7. Enter the valid OTP. 8. Tap Confirm. | Source account is debited by 1,000 + fee; beneficiary receives the funds; a transaction reference is displayed on the confirmation screen; a confirmation SMS is sent; the transaction appears in the customer's transaction history with correct details. |
| TC-02 | Per-transaction limit boundary — exactly at 10,000 | Customer has a source account with balance ≥ 10,000 + fee; one active beneficiary; daily limit not exhausted | 1. Navigate to Local Fund Transfer. 2. Select source account. 3. Select beneficiary. 4. Enter amount of exactly 10,000.00. 5. Complete OTP and confirm. | Transfer succeeds. Source account debited by 10,000 + fee. Confirmation screen and SMS displayed. |
| TC-03 | Per-transaction limit boundary — one unit over 10,000 | Same preconditions as TC-02 | 1. Enter amount of 10,000.01. 2. Complete OTP and confirm. | Transfer is rejected with a clear limit-exceeded message. Source account is NOT debited. No SMS is sent. Daily limit is NOT consumed. |
| TC-04 | Daily limit cumulative — reach exactly 20,000 across transfers | Customer has one source account with sufficient balance; one active beneficiary; no prior transfers today | 1. Transfer 15,000 (succeeds). 2. Initiate a second transfer of 5,000 to the same or different beneficiary. 3. Complete OTP and confirm. | Second transfer succeeds. Total debited today = 20,000 + fees. Confirmation shown. |
| TC-05 | Daily limit exceeded — one unit over 20,000 cumulative | Same as TC-04, but the second transfer is for 5,001 | 1. After the 15,000 transfer, initiate a transfer of 5,001. 2. Complete OTP and confirm. | Transfer is rejected with a clear daily-limit-exceeded message. Source account is NOT debited. No SMS. |
| TC-06 | Insufficient funds — amount exceeds available balance | Customer has a source account with balance of 500; one active beneficiary | 1. Enter transfer amount of 1,000. 2. Complete OTP and confirm. | Transfer is rejected with an insufficient-funds message. Source account is NOT debited. No SMS. |
| TC-07 | Insufficient funds — atomicity: amount + fee exceeds balance | Customer has source account with balance of 1,000; fee for this transfer is 50; one active beneficiary | 1. Enter transfer amount of 980 (which plus 50 fee = 1,030, exceeding the 1,000 balance). 2. Complete OTP and confirm. | Transfer is rejected. No debit occurs. The account balance remains 1,000. The rejection message indicates insufficient funds (not a generic error). |
| TC-08 | Invalid OTP — wrong code entered | Customer has initiated a transfer and is at the OTP entry screen | 1. Enter an OTP code that does not match the one sent. 2. Tap Confirm. | Transfer is NOT confirmed. An "invalid OTP" message is shown. The transfer remains in the pending-confirmation state. The customer can retry. |
| TC-09 | Expired OTP — enter OTP after expiry window | Customer initiated a transfer; OTP is allowed to expire (e.g., wait 6 minutes if expiry is 5 minutes) | 1. Enter the original OTP after expiry. 2. Tap Confirm. | Transfer is NOT confirmed. An "OTP expired" message is shown. The customer must request a new OTP. The original transfer is still cancellable. |
| TC-10 | OTP replay — captured OTP from one transfer applied to a different transfer | Customer initiates Transfer A (amount X, beneficiary A) and receives OTP-A; initiates Transfer B (amount Y, beneficiary B) and receives OTP-B | 1. Enter OTP-A when confirming Transfer B. 2. Tap Confirm. | Transfer B is NOT confirmed. An "invalid OTP" or "OTP does not match this transfer" message is shown. Transfer A's OTP remains valid only for Transfer A. No debit occurs on Transfer B. |
| TC-11 | Idempotency — double-tap on Confirm during OTP submission | Customer has entered the correct OTP and taps Confirm rapidly twice (or taps, and the app retries) | 1. Enter correct OTP. 2. Tap Confirm twice in quick succession (or trigger a retry of the same confirmation request). | Only ONE debit occurs. Only ONE transaction reference is generated. The second request returns the same result as the first (or a "already processed" response). No duplicate SMS is sent. The customer sees one confirmation screen. |
| TC-12 | Beneficiary blocked/deleted during OTP window | Customer has initiated a transfer to beneficiary B and is at the OTP screen; beneficiary B is deactivated or deleted by another channel (admin action or the customer's own action in another session) before OTP confirmation | 1. After beneficiary B is deactivated, enter the valid OTP for the transfer. 2. Tap Confirm. | Transfer is rejected with a clear message indicating the beneficiary is no longer valid. Source account is NOT debited. No SMS is sent. The transfer can be cancelled or re-initiated to a different beneficiary. |

### Why these 12

These 12 cases are not a sample of "everything that could happen." They are the
cases that target the highest-risk root causes identified in Section 1.2 and the
questions in Section 1.1. TC-02 and TC-03 test the per-transaction limit
boundary on both sides. TC-04 and TC-05 test cumulative daily limit and reset.
TC-06 and TC-07 separate insufficient-funds from insufficient-funds-plus-fee —
a common source of atomicity bugs. TC-08, TC-09, and TC-10 together cover the
OTP lifecycle: wrong code, expiry, and replay across transfers. TC-11 is the
idempotency case and is the single most important financial-risk case in the set.
TC-12 tests the concurrency edge case where beneficiary state changes mid-flow.
TC-01 is the happy path and establishes the baseline. No two cases here test the
same root cause.

## 1.4 Coverage Decisions

### What I deliberately did NOT cover and why

- **UI layout, typography, colour, and responsive behaviour.** Cosmetic and
  UX-polish issues do not create financial or regulatory risk. They are
  important in production but are not the focus of this assessment.
- **Performance and load.** The brief focuses on functional correctness. Load
  testing requires a separate environment, tooling, and agreement on
  thresholds.
- **Security and penetration testing.** Explicitly excluded by the assessment
  brief. I flag security-relevant risks in the risk assessment (OTP replay,
  idempotency) but do not perform active security testing.
- **Cross-currency and FX.** The Local Fund Transfer requirement describes a
  local transfer. FX is addressed separately in Task 3.
- **SMS gateway internal behaviour.** I test the integration point (was a
  request sent? did the transfer commit regardless?) but not the gateway's
  delivery mechanics. That is the provider's responsibility.
- **Accessibility (a11y).** Important in production but not in scope for this
  assessment and would require tooling I have not set up here.
- **Compatibility matrix.** Assumes the team has a defined supported-matrix
  document. I would test the critical path on the top 2–3 combinations if
  given a specific matrix.
- **Full beneficiary lifecycle.** I test that a blocked/deleted beneficiary
  blocks the transfer (TC-12). I do not test the full add/edit/verify/
  deactivate lifecycle of beneficiaries — that is a separate feature.

### Automated vs manual

| Case | Suite | Rationale |
|------|-------|-----------|
| TC-01 (happy path) | Automated regression | Core path; must pass every build. Stable preconditions, clear assertions on balance change, reference generation, and transaction history. |
| TC-02 (per-txn limit at boundary) | Automated regression | Boundary case; deterministic given test data. Assert transfer succeeds at exactly 10,000. |
| TC-03 (per-txn limit over boundary) | Automated regression | Boundary case; deterministic. Assert rejection and no debit. |
| TC-04 (daily limit cumulative) | Automated regression | Depends on sequence of two transfers; deterministic if test data sets up the first transfer. Assert total reaches exactly 20,000. |
| TC-05 (daily limit over cumulative) | Automated regression | Deterministic rejection case. Assert no debit and correct error. |
| TC-06 (insufficient funds) | Automated regression | Deterministic given test data. Assert rejection and balance unchanged. |
| TC-07 (insufficient funds + fee atomicity) | Automated regression | The fee-included boundary is the key assertion. Deterministic given known fee. Assert no debit and balance unchanged. |
| TC-08 (invalid OTP) | Automated regression | Can be automated with a known invalid OTP code. Assert rejection and retry-availability. |
| TC-09 (expired OTP) | Manual or time-gated automated | Requires waiting for expiry. Automatable with a sleep or by manipulating the server clock in a test environment, but the wait makes it flaky under CI. Prefer manual or a dedicated time-manipulation test hook. |
| TC-10 (OTP replay across transfers) | Automated regression | Two transfers, two OTPs, one wrong OTP applied. Deterministic. Assert rejection on the second transfer. |
| TC-11 (idempotency / double-tap) | Automated regression + exploratory manual | The automated version sends the same confirmation request twice and asserts a single debit. Manual exploratory testing with real double-tap behaviour (network retries, UI double-tap) complements it. |
| TC-12 (beneficiary blocked during OTP window) | Manual exploratory | Requires coordinating a state change in another session or channel during the OTP window. Hard to make deterministic in automated CI without a dedicated test hook. Best as a targeted manual test, then automated once the hook exists. |

### How I would know this feature is safe to release

- All 11 automated regression cases pass in the CI pipeline on the release
  candidate, with no flaky failures.
- TC-09 (expired OTP) and TC-12 (beneficiary mid-flow state change) have been
  executed manually and pass — even if not yet automated, they have been
  covered.
- Idempotency (TC-11) has been tested both as an automated API-level duplicate
  request and as a manual double-tap/retry simulation. Both pass.
- Fee calculation has been verified against the bank's fee configuration for at
  least three amount bands and one rounding edge case, and the rounded values
  match the expected amounts in the ledger.
- Daily limit reset timing has been verified against the customer's local
  timezone, not UTC, by running a transfer just before and just after midnight.
- Error messages have been verified to be specific and distinct for each failure
  mode (invalid OTP, expired OTP, insufficient funds, limit exceeded, invalid
  beneficiary), not generic.
- Transaction reference numbers have been checked for uniqueness across at least
  50 consecutive transfers in the test environment, and the format matches the
  expected date-plus-sequence pattern.
- The SMS side-effect has been verified to fire after commit and to not block the
  commit if the SMS gateway is slow; a simulated SMS gateway failure does not
  roll back the transfer.
- No P1 or P2 defects are open against this feature. Any P3 defects are documented
  and accepted by the product owner.
- The feature has been briefly smoke-tested on the top two supported device/
  browser combinations from the team's compatibility matrix.
